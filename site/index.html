How it works

From a payment file to a paid refund

Four steps. You pay only when a refund actually arrives.

A 20-minute self-audit on your own computer. MFP Refund Check reads your MTF 835 payment files and shows expected refunds against payments, dollars by denial code and the days left on each claim. We see only the totals.

A one-page services agreement and a business associate agreement (BAA), the HIPAA contract you sign before an outside helper may see patient data. Nothing patient-level reaches us before both are signed.

You add us as a user on your Medicare Transaction Facilitator (MTF) account. We never ask for your password. We sort open claims by days left, then by dollars, and check each N907 against HRSA’s public 340B database (OPAIS).

We file good-faith inquiries (GFIs) in Beacon, the system drugmakers use, and answer their requests. With complete documents, drugmakers resolve GFIs in 3 to 10 days.5 When a refund lands on a later 835, we invoice 25% of it.

What we catch

Every way a refund goes missing

The free check flags each one on its own. Once you hire us, we follow it through.

  • Denied as 340B N907

    The drugmaker thinks you bought the drug at a 340B discount. With no 340B contract-pharmacy agreement, that denial is likely wrong.

    Shows up as

    • N907 remark
    • $0 refund

    What we do

    • OPAIS check
    • GFI in Beacon

  • Short-paid N909

    A non-standard amount was paid, so part of the expected refund, the Standard Default Refund Amount (SDRA), is missing.

    Shows up as

    • N909 remark
    • Below SDRA

    What we do

    • SDRA check
    • GFI in Beacon

  • No refund yet N910

    The drugmaker reports no refund at this time, so the money you expected is still outstanding.

    Shows up as

    • N910 remark
    • $0 refund

    What we do

    • Invoice proof
    • Follow-up

  • Plan data error N911

    A data problem on the Part D plan’s side is holding up the refund. It goes to the plan, not the drugmaker.

    Shows up as

    • N911 remark
    • Blocked refund

    What we do

    • Send to the plan
    • Follow-up

  • Taken back PLB WO

    A clawback shows up on a later 835 as a provider-level adjustment and shrinks a future payment.

    Shows up as

    • PLB code WO
    • Later 835

    What we do

    • Ledger check
    • Dispute

  • Paid late

    Even a correct refund lands after you have paid for the drug. CMS reports about 21 days after the fill, on average.6

    Shows up as

    • Days waiting
    • Cash squeeze

    What we do

    • Deadline queue
    • Monthly report

The stakes

Pharmacies pay first and wait to be paid back

You buy the drug at about its list price (WAC). The Medicare plan pays you based on the lower Maximum Fair Price (MFP). The drugmaker owes you the difference, the SDRA, within 14 days of getting the claim data.1

Questions

What owners ask first

Do you need my MTF password?

No. You add us as a user on your MTF account, so we never ask for or use your password.

What do you see during the free check?

Only totals. The check runs on your computer and the claim lines stay there. It can also export a de-identified summary, which is safe to share before a BAA.

What if nothing is recovered?

You owe nothing. The fee is 25% of refunds actually paid on claims we disputed.

Is this a government program?

No. MFP Refund Recovery is an independent company. We are not affiliated with CMS or the Medicare Transaction Facilitator.

We have 340B contracts. Does this still help?

An N907 denial may be correct for your pharmacy. Short pays (N909 and N910), clawbacks and late refunds still count, and the free check shows all of them.

Doesn’t my PSAO already do this?

Ask them. Some pharmacy services administrative organizations (PSAOs) may. We can work alongside yours.

Can I stop at any time?

Yes. Either side can end the agreement with 30 days’ notice.
