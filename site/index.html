The problem

Your pharmacy pays first and waits to be paid back

Here is the path the money takes on one eligible fill, and where it goes missing.

  1. You →to wholesaler

    You buy the drug at about its list price, the wholesale acquisition cost (WAC).

  2. Part D plan →to you

    The patient’s Medicare drug plan pays you based on the much lower Maximum Fair Price (MFP).

  3. MTF →to drugmaker

    The Medicare Transaction Facilitator (MTF), a system run by CMS, sends the claim to the drugmaker.

  4. Drugmaker

    The drugmaker must pay your refund within 14 days of getting the claim data. The default amount is the Standard Default Refund Amount (SDRA), which is WAC − MFP.1

  5. Refund →to you

    You get the money plus an 835 file that explains the payment claim by claim. On average, refunds reach pharmacies about 21 days after the fill.3

Four ways a refund goes wrong

Denied N907
The drugmaker thinks the drug was bought at a 340B discount, the deep discount a federal program gives certain hospitals and clinics. If your pharmacy has no 340B contract-pharmacy agreement, that denial is likely wrong.
Short or unpaid N909 · N910
N909 means a non-standard amount was paid. N910 means no refund at this time. Either way, part of the refund is missing.
Taken back PLB WO
A clawback appears on a later 835 as a provider-level adjustment and shrinks a future payment.
Late about 21 days
Even a correct refund lands after you have already paid for the drug.3 The money isn’t lost, but it squeezes your cash.

120 daysto dispute each claim

Each dispute is a good-faith inquiry (GFI), filed one claim at a time in the Resolution Center of Beacon, the system drugmakers use. It must be filed within 120 days of the 835.4 With complete documents, drugmakers resolve GFIs in 3 to 10 days.5

    We sort open claims by days left, then by dollars, and check each N907 against OPAIS, the public 340B database run by HRSA.

    We file GFIs in Beacon, or prepare each one for you to submit if your account requires it.

    When a refund lands on a later 835, we invoice 25% of it.

    One page showing what was recovered, what is pending, and what was denied and why.
